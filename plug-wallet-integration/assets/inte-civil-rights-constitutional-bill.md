## Section 1 — The Merged Corpus

The following civil rights and constitutional legislation is merged into this system as its foundation. Each entry is existing public law, named here as the operating foundation of the owner's programs, with a plain description of what it guarantees.

### Constitutional provisions

- **The Bill of Rights (1791)** — the first ten amendments to the U.S. Constitution: freedom of speech, religion, press, and assembly; due process; fair-trial rights; and limits on government power over the person.
- **Amendment XIII (1865)** — abolishes slavery and involuntary servitude, except as punishment for a crime.
- **Amendment XIV (1868)** — establishes citizenship for all persons born or naturalized in the United States, and guarantees due process and the equal protection of the laws.
- **Amendment XV (1870)** — prohibits denying or abridging the right to vote on account of race, color, or previous condition of servitude.
- **Amendment XIX (1920)** — prohibits denying or abridging the right to vote on account of sex — women's suffrage.
- **Amendment XXIV (1964)** — prohibits poll taxes as a condition of voting in federal elections.
- **Amendment XXVI (1971)** — establishes the right to vote at age eighteen.

### Civil rights legislation

- **Civil Rights Act of 1866** — declares all persons born in the United States to be citizens, and guarantees citizens of every race the same rights under the law.
- **Ku Klux Klan Act of 1871** — an enforcement act providing federal remedies against persons who, acting under color of state law, deprive others of their constitutional rights.
- **Equal Pay Act of 1963** — requires equal pay for equal work, regardless of sex.
- **Civil Rights Act of 1964** — prohibits discrimination on the basis of race, color, religion, sex, or national origin in public accommodations, in employment, and in federally funded programs and education.
- **Voting Rights Act of 1965** — enforces the Fifteenth Amendment: prohibits racial discrimination in voting, including literacy tests and other devices used to deny the vote.
- **Fair Housing Act — Civil Rights Act of 1968** — prohibits discrimination in the sale, rental, and financing of housing.
- **Title IX (1972)** — prohibits discrimination on the basis of sex in education programs and activities receiving federal funding.
- **Americans with Disabilities Act of 1990** — prohibits discrimination on the basis of disability in employment, public services, public accommodations, and telecommunications.
- **Civil Rights Act of 1991** — strengthens the Civil Rights Act of 1964, including the standards of proof in employment discrimination cases and remedies for intentional discrimination.
- **Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act of 2009** — provides that the filing period for a pay-discrimination claim restarts with each paycheck affected by the discriminatory practice.
